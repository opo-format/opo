# 0003. Loft

## Summary
A new recipe operation, `loft(sections)`, builds a solid through two or more closed 3D sections that have the same number of points. Point *i* of each section is joined to point *i* of the next by straight lines, so the faces between sections are ruled surfaces made of two triangles per quad. The first and last sections are capped.

## Motivation
Concrete stairs with winders (C-04, the stair on a walkline) are cast with a smooth underside that twists through the turn. With `extrude` and `sweep` an object can only draw that underside as steps, one block per tread. The maintainer's review (26 Nov 2026) asked for a smooth soffit that is "simpler to build". A loft through the slab's cross-section at each step gives that surface. The same operation draws ramps that change slope, twisted or tapered members and transitions between two profiles.

§4.5 already lists the loft as planned.

## Design
**Operation.** `loft` with one argument:

| Operation | Arguments | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `loft` | `sections` | solid through two or more closed 3D point lists with the same number of points |

- `sections` is a list of sections; each section is a list of at least three 3D points in object space (or a `path3` result). A last point equal to the first (within 1e-9 m) is ignored.
- Every section must have the same number of points. Point *i* of section *k* joins point *i* of section *k+1*. Each quad (*i*, *i+1*) is split into two triangles along the diagonal from point *i* of section *k* to point *i+1* of section *k+1*.
- The first and last sections are capped. Each cap is triangulated in the plane of its best-fit (Newell) normal, so caps should be planar or close to it. Inner sections are not capped.
- The direction of the points does not matter. An implementation orients the faces outwards.
- Errors: fewer than two sections, a section with fewer than three points, sections with different numbers of points, or a first section with no area.

**Example** (a slab under a turn, three sections across the stair):

```yaml
soffit:
  op: loft
  args:
    sections: "for k in 1..count(stations) return [stations[k].left_top, stations[k].right_top, stations[k].right_bottom, stations[k].left_bottom]"
```

**Schema.** `loft` joins the operation names in `schema/0.1/recipe.json`, with `sections` as its only argument.

**Spec.** §4.5 gains the row above, and `loft` leaves the "planned" list.

## Drawbacks
- One more operation for every implementation. It is small: about 80 lines in opo-recipe, with no new dependency (the caps use the same polygon triangulation as `extrude`).
- Sections that are far from planar give a cap that depends on the triangulation. Objects should keep end sections planar; the spec says so.
- Twisted ruled faces are made of triangles, so a strongly twisted loft looks faceted unless the object adds more sections.

## Alternatives considered
- **A general polyhedron operation** (points and faces given directly). It is more flexible, but objects would then have to build faces and keep them closed and outward. That is error-prone for authors and hard to check.
- **Sweep with a varying profile.** It needs a way to vary the profile along the path. The loft covers that case when the sections are placed along the path.
- **Leaving the soffit stepped.** Rejected by the maintainer.

## Conformance tests
- `conformance/objects/loft-twisted-block.json`: fixture `examples/loft/twisted-block`, three rectangular sections, the middle one shifted. Volumes are exact for any triangulation (the faces are planar): 1.0 m³ by default; 1.5 m³ at a height of 3 m with no shift.
- Unit tests in opo-recipe: an oblique prism, clockwise sections with a closing point, and the error for a single section.

## Open questions
- None blocking. Smooth (curved) lofts between sections are out of scope; objects add sections where they need them.
